Welcome

I recently completed a Postdoctoral Research Fellowship at Worcester Polytechnic Institute, where my research focused on LLM evaluation, trustworthy and responsible AI, and privacy-preserving classroom analytics. One area of my work explored how LLMs can be used to identify potentially confusing, ambiguous, or misinterpreted parts of health messages — helping us understand how people may perceive health information and ultimately design more effective health communication. Alongside this work, I studied privacy-preserving approaches for analyzing multimodal classroom data while protecting the identities of individuals represented in the data.

I will be joining Syracuse University's ABC Innovation Lab as a postdoctoral researcher on a USDA-funded project developing agentic AI data services for producer-facing markets. Before WPI, I spent three years at Moody's Analytics building production LLM and NLP systems for financial risk analytics.

I hold a Ph.D. in Data Science from Worcester Polytechnic Institute, where my dissertation research developed self-supervised autoencoder methods for recovering signal from missing and corrupted data.

Research Areas

LLM Evaluation

Evaluating how different approaches — zero-shot prompting, retrieval-augmented generation, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and prompt optimization — affect the quality and accuracy of LLM-generated answers, with evaluation focused on task-specific performance rather than fluency alone. A related thread uses LLM surprisal to identify potentially confusing or difficult-to-process language in health messages, and evaluates whether agentic rewriting can improve message clarity and human comprehension.

Trustworthy & Responsible AI

Studying how AI systems can remain reliable when information is incomplete, corrupted, or transformed, including methods for recovering useful signal without requiring fully observed ground-truth data and approaches to evaluating AI-generated content from multiple perspectives.

Privacy-Preserving Analytics

Studying the privacy–informational utility tradeoff directly rather than assuming anonymization is cost-free — including how much identifying information can be removed from video or voice data while preserving information that people need to interpret the underlying activity.
